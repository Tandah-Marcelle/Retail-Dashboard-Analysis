### 1.2 Key Observation: Margins Are Strikingly Uniform

The range across all regions is **40.43% (East) to 41.04% (Adamawa)** — a spread of just 0.61 percentage points. This is statistically near-identical.

This uniformity is both a finding and an implication:
- **Discount policy is being applied consistently nationwide** — no regional manager is making independent pricing decisions that deviate significantly
- **The margin problem is systemic, not geographic** — there is no one "bad region" dragging the average down; the entire network is affected equally
- **Geographic diversification is working** — NorthPeak is not over-reliant on any single region

## 2. Volume Concentration — The Two-Tier Structure

There is a clear two-tier volume structure in NorthPeak's regional footprint:

**Tier 1 — High-Volume Regions (9,000–10,100 transactions)**
- North-West, Littoral, South-West, Centre

**Tier 2 — Lower-Volume Regions (~5,000 transactions)**
- Far-North, North, Adamawa, East

The Tier 1 regions generate roughly **twice the transaction volume** of Tier 2 regions. This is likely explained by population density and economic activity concentration in Cameroon's urban centers (Douala, Yaoundé, Bamenda, Buea).

## 3. Regional Recommendations

### 3.1 Tier 1 Regions — Protect and Optimize

North-West, Littoral, South-West, and Centre are the revenue backbone of NorthPeak. They collectively account for approximately **57% of total transactions and revenue**.

**Action R1: Maintain Inventory Depth in Tier 1**
High transaction volumes in these regions mean stockouts are costly. Ensure consistent availability of high-margin products — particularly Office Supplies (Accessories, Binders) which sell consistently without requiring deep discounts.

**Action R2: Introduce Loyalty Programs in Tier 1 Urban Centers**
Douala (Littoral) and Yaoundé (Centre) likely represent the highest concentration of Corporate and Home Office customers. A loyalty card or corporate account program in these cities would:
- Reduce the need for ad-hoc sales discounts (salespeople use discounts because they have no other retention tool)
- Improve repeat purchase rates
- Provide better customer data for future targeting

**Action R3: North-West is the Top Profit Region — Investigate Why**
North-West leads in both transactions (10,085) and total profit (2.56 billion FCFA). Bamenda driving this volume may reflect a specific customer mix or product preference worth understanding. **Recommendation:** Conduct a qualitative survey of North-West sales reps to identify replicable best practices.

---

### 3.2 Tier 2 Regions — Growth Opportunity

Far-North, North, Adamawa, and East each generate roughly 5,000 transactions — half the volume of Tier 1. Their margins, however, are essentially the same. This means **profitability per transaction is consistent** — there is no efficiency loss in operating in these regions.

**Action R4: Understand What Limits Volume in Tier 2**
The lower transaction counts likely reflect:
- Smaller population centers (Maroua, Garoua, Ngaoundéré, Bertoua)
- Lower corporate customer density
- Potentially less aggressive sales coverage

**Recommended investigation:** Are these regions under-staffed, under-inventoried, or simply lower-demand markets? Data alone cannot answer this — but the margins suggest the business model works there; volume is the constraint.

**Action R5: Test Online Channel Expansion in Tier 2 Regions**
Currently, online sales only exist in 4 regions: Littoral, Centre, South-West, and North-West. Tier 2 regions — particularly North and Far-North — are served exclusively by physical stores. Given the distances customers may travel to physical stores in these regions, online ordering could meaningfully increase volume without adding store infrastructure costs.

## 4. Channel Strategy — Physical Store vs. Online

### 4.1 Current Channel State

The dashboard shows **Online vs. Physical Store Margin: 40.6%** — essentially identical performance across channels. However, this analysis is based on only 4 online regions vs. 10 physical store regions. Online is under-represented in the data.

### 4.2 Why Online Expansion Makes Strategic Sense

| Factor | Physical Store | Online |
|--------|----------------|--------|
| Geographic reach | Limited to city | National |
| Operating cost | High (rent, staff) | Lower |
| Discount pressure | Higher (face-to-face sales) | Lower (self-service) |
| Inventory exposure | Per-location | Centralized |
| Margin potential | Current: ~40.6% | Equal or better |

In physical retail environments, salespeople have more opportunity to offer discounts in the moment to close a deal. Online channels typically enforce list pricing with limited discount flexibility — which may naturally reduce deep-discount transactions.

**Action R6: Expand Online to All 10 Regions**
Add online geography entries for: West (Bafoussam), North (Garoua), Far-North (Maroua), Adamawa (Ngaoundéré), East (Bertoua), South (Ebolowa). This doubles online coverage without opening a single new store.

**Action R7: Test Online-Exclusive Pricing**
Run a 90-day test: offer consistent 5% online pricing advantage (vs. physical store list price) with **no additional discounts**. This could:
- Drive physical-to-online channel shift (lower operating cost)
- Reduce ad-hoc discounting in physical stores
- Create a measurable A/B comparison of channel margin performance

## 5. Customer Segment by Region — Strategic Opportunity

The donut chart from the dashboard shows:
- **Consumer: 55.12%** of revenue
- **Corporate: 29.36%** of revenue
- **Home Office: 15.52%** of revenue

All three segments perform at near-identical margins (~40.5–40.7%). This means **segment mix is not currently a margin driver** — which is expected given that the discount rate is applied uniformly regardless of segment.

However, this creates an opportunity:

**Action R8: Differentiate Pricing by Segment**
- **Corporate accounts** can be offered pre-negotiated contract pricing (e.g., 8% fixed discount on all orders above a minimum annual spend threshold). This replaces ad-hoc discounting with structured pricing.
- **Consumer segment** should have minimal discounting — they are impulse or need-based buyers who are less price-sensitive in B2C retail contexts.
- **Home Office** is an underserved segment at 15.52%. These are likely freelancers, SME owners, and remote workers. Consider targeted bundles (Laptop + Chair + Desk) marketed as a "Home Office Setup Package" — driving higher basket sizes without heavy individual-product discounting.
